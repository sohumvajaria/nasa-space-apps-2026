How to listen

Use headphones. Move with ←↑↓→ (or WASD). You always face up (north). A soft pulse beats steadily from Null Island (0, 0), where the equator meets the prime meridian (the glowing orb on the map), and you can walk anywhere on Earth: it gets quieter with distance and pans to show which side it is on, so you can always find your way back. An instrument plays the data under you: violin for rain, cello for land heat, flute for plants, clarinet for ocean heat and French horn for haze. The more intense the data where you stand, the louder and brighter it plays. Press 6 for the Earth Orchestra: all five at once, so every place on Earth has its own chord. Press H for a short summary of what is playing and where you are (for example "Heavy rain. France, Europe."), and L to hear which layer you are on. Use the sound key to preview each level. Switch layers with 1 to 6; each switch is announced and plays that instrument. V switches between the 3D terrain and the flat map. Hold Shift to move faster, M to mute. When the data reaches its most extreme level (extreme rain, scorching land, very warm water, thick smoke), the instrument trembles, the way an orchestra's strings shiver to signal danger. Press T for Then & now: hear the same day 20 years earlier, then press T again to come back. If you use a screen reader, set Voice to "screen reader" so announcements come only through your own reader.
