Earth Jukebox

NASA's Earth data, made to be heard. Move across a map with your keyboard, voice or finger, and listen to what is there.

The idea

How it works

Most satellite maps are pictures, which leaves out anyone who cannot see them. Earth Jukebox turns real NASA satellite data into sound you can move through, with headphones and any of a keyboard, a voice or a touch screen.

  1. Pick a layer: rain, land heat, plants, ocean heat or haze, or the Earth Information Center's year of ocean temperatures.
  2. Move across the map. Where the data is stronger, the sound changes: louder, brighter or higher, depending on the page.
  3. Ask for a spoken summary any time, or ask the guide to take you somewhere: "the warmest water", "the heaviest rain", "Tokyo".

There are two ways in, built on the same data. Explore a place drops you into one region as a 3D landscape. World navigator lets you roam the whole planet and be guided to places by sound.

Data

Six layers of NASA data

Layers come live from NASA GIBS, plus one dataset from NASA's Earth Information Center. Colors in each image are converted back to values using that layer's own NASA legend.

LayerNASA datasetWhat you hear
RainGPM IMERG precipitation rateExplore: a minor sixth that grows louder and brighter in heavier rain. World: pitch rises with the rain.
Land heatMODIS Terra land surface temperatureExplore: a perfect fifth with a shimmer that burns brighter on hotter ground. Oceans are silent.
PlantsMODIS Terra vegetation index (NDVI)Explore: a major third that swells slowly, fuller over dense forest.
Ocean heatGHRSST MUR sea surface temperatureExplore: a perfect fourth that drifts and warms with the water. Land is silent.
HazeMODIS Terra aerosol optical depthExplore: a tense tritone that pulses unevenly, harsher in thick smoke or dust.
Ocean year (EIC)Earth Information Center, ECCO2 sea surface temperature (NASA SVS #3912)World: pitch rises with warmer water through a full year of seasons, March 2007 to February 2008. Land is silent.

Satellites cannot see through clouds on some layers, so heat, plants and haze have gaps. Gaps are silent. The daily layers go back 30 days from the newest day NASA has published; they are observed data, so there are no forecasts.

Listening

What you hear

  • Spatial audioBinaural 3D sound (HRTF) from the browser's Web Audio engine. With headphones you can tell left from right, and ahead from behind.
  • Intensity becomes soundStronger data is louder and brighter (Explore) or higher in pitch (World). Each layer has clearly separate levels, named in plain words like light, moderate, heavy and extreme.
  • Sound keyOn the Explore page, buttons play each level on demand so you can learn what the sounds mean before you move.
  • Three instrumentsA warm synth drone (the default), a soft pulse (one round, gentle tone per beat), and in World a pure tone. Switch any time.
Explore a place

A landscape you can walk through

  • 3D terrain viewIntensity becomes height, so a storm is a mountain, with topographic contour lines and drifting light. A glowing beam marks the sound source and the camera follows you from behind, facing north, the way the sound is oriented. V switches to a flat map.
  • HotspotsEach layer is centered on a point of interest: the heaviest storm of the day, the hottest ground, the Amazon, the western Pacific warm pool, the haziest air.
  • Spoken summary on demandPress H for a short line like "Heavy rain. Storm center ahead left, 14 units away."
  • Date sliderGo back up to 30 days. Layers are cached, so switching is instant.
World navigator

The whole planet, guided by ear

  • Roam everywhereA latitude and longitude cursor over a 3D globe or a flat map. The equator and the prime meridian are the two axes, and (0, 0) is home. Chimes tell you when you cross them.
  • Guided to a targetA metronome ticks faster as you get closer, and it comes from the direction of the target, with higher ticks for north and lower for south. A rising chord means you arrived.
  • Ask the guideType or speak: "take me to Tokyo", "go to the warmest water", "show me the rain", "why is it cold here?", "turn off the chimes". Simple requests run on your device. Open questions go to Claude, with answers grounded in the data under your cursor.
  • Voice and screen readersIt speaks your position, place, value and date when you stop. Everything spoken is also in a live region for screen readers.
  • Play the yearOn the ocean year layer, press P to hear a year of seasons pass by at your spot. [ and ] step through dates on any layer.
  • SettingsAbout twenty saved settings: musical scale, pitch range, metronome speed and sound, voice speed, and more. The guide can change them for you.
Design

Beautiful, calm and accessible

  • A real 3D EarthBuilt from NASA imagery: relief by day, city lights by night, a warm twilight line, a glint on the sea, and an atmosphere that glows with the sun. The data layer wraps around it, and the glow swells as the data under you gets more intense. Drag to spin it.
  • Touch controlsPress and drag on a map like a joystick: a little to walk, farther to go faster. Phones can vibrate when you move into stronger data.
  • Never requiredThe visuals are decoration and navigation aids. Everything works with sound, a keyboard and a screen reader alone. Motion eases off if your system asks for reduced motion.
  • Real NASA data, liveMaps are fetched from NASA when you open the page, not recorded in advance.
